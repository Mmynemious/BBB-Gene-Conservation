My reading

This paper is about the immune genes. The title is Immune Genes are Hotspots of Shared Positive Selection Across Birds and Mammals, by Allison J. Shultz and Timothy Sackton at Harvard University.

What they did is they assumed that there is a common selective pressure across different taxa, and they used alignments of orthologous protein coding genes from 39 species of birds to estimate positive selection across 11,000 genes conserved across birds. They also used pathogen challenge transcriptome data. They noticed that genes upregulated in, I do not know what exactly, response to pathogens are also enriched for positively selected genes. Proteins that interact with viruses experience about two times as many amino acid changes compared to proteins that do not.

Birds are used since they share convergent features with mammals, which will be important later on, because they can actually cross-reference the enrichment pathways of positively selected genes across species.

The methods include comparative genomics in birds, in which they did a genome-wide scan of positive selection without any a priori assumptions of gene function. This is a strong starting point, because it makes it very clear later on whether their assumption about positively selected genes and the immune system is correct or not.

They found four categories where positive selection is high: the immune system, the recombination and replication genes, lipid metabolism genes, and phototransduction genes. Their key hypothesis is that adaptation against pathogens consistently involves the same genes across evolutionary timescales, which again allows for the cross-reference of enrichment of pathways.

They found out that there are strong signatures of positive selection throughout the avian genome. They used PAML and HyPhy. They used a gene tree as an input tree, and then for 8,669 genes they used a species tree as an input tree. They used the dN/dS parameter, I believe, and they also used BUSTED, which, yeah, maybe not too important for me.

So the immune system, recombination, lipid metabolism and phototransduction pathways are enriched for positive selection in birds. Genes under positive selection have significantly larger overall differential expression effect values compared to those only under positive selection in birds or those not under positive selection.

Pathogens drive shared signatures of selection across birds and mammals. There is a strong overlap in genes with positive selection, and viruses produce the strongest signature of pathogen mediated selection.

What I take from it

The scan without any a priori assumptions of gene function is a strong starting point, because it makes it very clear whether their assumption about positively selected genes and the immune system is correct or not. And using birds allows them to cross-reference the enrichment pathways of positively selected genes across species.

Checks against the paper

  • Upregulated in response to what? Pathogen challenge. They reanalysed 12 bird studies covering seven pathogens: four viruses, two bacteria and one protist. Genes upregulated after infection were more likely to be under positive selection than genes not differentially expressed.
  • Two times as many amino acid changes for virus interacting proteins? That is a result from mammals that they cite, Enard et al. 2016, not a finding of this paper.
  • Genome-wide GWAS? A genome-wide scan, not a GWAS. A GWAS links variants to a trait within one species; this scans every gene for selection across species.
  • 11,000 genes, and 8,669 on the species tree? Yes. 11,231 genes with the gene tree, 8,669 with the species tree. The species tree set only kept alignments with one sequence per species; the gene tree set allowed up to three paralogs.
  • The dN/dS parameter, I believe? Yes. All the PAML site models and BUSTED test whether some sites have dN/dS above 1. 14% of genes passed every test.
  • Four categories? Yes. 18 KEGG pathways were significantly enriched, and they fall into immune, recombination and replication, lipid metabolism and phototransduction.
  • Larger differential expression effect values? The comparison has three groups: genes under selection in both birds and mammals, in birds only, or in neither. The genes under selection in both had the largest effect values, mostly in response to viruses.
  • Strong overlap between birds and mammals? Yes, significant at every cutoff they tried, on 4,931 genes with results in both. It held after removing the most constrained genes and accounting for alignment length.

Open questions from this read

  • BUSTED, again marked as not too important for me. Second paper in a row that relies on it.

How it relates to this project

  1. It uses the same measure. dN/dS on aligned coding sequence, across a whole genome, is the conservation measure this project uses on its BBB genes.

  2. It shows the one sequence per species choice. The species tree analysis kept only alignments with one sequence per species, the same restriction as the one-to-one orthologue decision in this project.

  3. It pairs with Nandakumar 2025. Both papers find immune genes enriched for positive selection, one across birds and mammals and one within rodents, and both lean on BUSTED.

Reference detail from the paper

  • Proportions under positive selection (gene trees, 11,231 genes): M1a vs M2a 17%, M7 vs M8 67%, M8 vs M8a 33%, BUSTED 56%, all tests 1,562 genes (14%). Species trees: 1,203 of 8,669 (14%) in all tests.
  • Overall ω (M0 model): mean 0.15, median 0.10. Median 0.344 for genes under selection against 0.084 for the rest.
  • Alignment length: longer genes were more often under selection, likely more power to detect it.
  • Pathway enrichment: 351 selected genes and 3,347 background genes mapped to KEGG with chicken as reference; 18 pathways enriched at q < 0.1.
  • Bird and mammal transcriptomes: significant overlap in upregulated genes for influenza A, West Nile virus, E. coli and Mycoplasma; none in downregulated genes for viruses or bacteria.
  • Classic host pathogen genes under selection in both clades and upregulated after infection: PKR, PARP9, MX1.
