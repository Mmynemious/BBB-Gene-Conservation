Literature Review

Papers on BBB gene conservation, translational models, and comparative genomics — with comments

Author

Yara Ismail

Published

September 23, 2026

Papers are read against six fields, in the order below, so entries stay comparable. Each paper has its own page — the card links through. Terms are collected in the Methods Glossary; the papers the pipeline itself is built on are cited on the Resources page.

How each paper is read

1. Year published
Establishes what was technically possible at the time. A 2010 microarray study and a 2025 deep-proteomics study are not making the same kind of claim.
2. Model(s) used
Which species, whether the comparison is within or across species, and whether the cross-species claim rests on the same tissue.
3. Brain region
Recorded whenever stated. Cerebrovascular endothelium differs between brain regions, and between arterial, capillary and venous segments of the same vessel tree, so a gene “BBB-enriched” in one region or segment may not be in another. Where a paper does not state the region — or where regions are not matched between species — that is recorded too.
4. What is measured
DNA, RNA, or protein. Sequence conservation and expression answer different questions, and a paper measuring RNA cannot on its own support a claim about conservation.
5. How it is measured
Microarray, bulk RNA-seq, single-cell/nucleus RNA-seq, proteomics, genomic sequencing. For RNA-seq, bulk or single-cell, and whether cells were sorted. Bulk brain tissue drowns endothelial signal in neurons; isolated microvessels or sorted endothelium do not.
6. Statistical and modelling techniques
What was done to compare across species — orthologue assignment, differential tests and thresholds, alignment, selection estimates, phylogenetic versus pairwise, multiple-testing correction.

Papers

Song et al. 2020

2020 Human · mouse RNA Laser capture microdissection

Brain microvessels cut straight out of frozen tissue by laser, so low abundance transcripts survive and nothing is dissociated. The transporter families are the story: Slco1c1 and Slc22a8 mouse-enriched, SLCO1A2 human-only, and a worked example of a species difference in one transporter breaking a mouse disease model. Also a first molecular definition of human brain pericytes.

Miao et al. 2026

2026 Mouse · human RNA Single-cell and single-nucleus

The closest paper yet to what this project is doing, published two months ago. Same two species and the same question of how far mouse stands in for human at the barrier, but measured through expression rather than coding sequence. 169 genes enriched in human capillary endothelium, 386 in mouse, and the family that diverges most is the SLC transporters, a block this project’s gene list carries 52 of.

Read the entry →

O’Brown et al. 2018

2018 Fly · shark · zebrafish · mouse · human Review Comparative

A comparative review of the barrier across organisms, from Ehrlich’s dye experiments to iPSC models, with zebrafish and mouse compared in detail. It makes this project’s central claim, that barrier machinery is conserved across species, from expression and function rather than sequence. It also reports regional differences in permeability within the mouse brain, and states that barrier properties are acquired from the neural microenvironment rather than being intrinsic to the endothelial cell.

Read the entry →

Park et al. 2019

2019 Human only, in vitro Barrier function Organ-on-a-chip

A human blood brain barrier built on a two channel microfluidic chip, from iPSC-derived endothelium differentiated under hypoxia and interfaced with primary human astrocytes and pericytes. It holds barrier function for a week, shows working efflux pumps rather than merely present ones, and delivers a clinically approved antibody across the barrier by reversible osmotic opening. No cross-species comparison at all, which is the point: it is an attempt to skip the species jump entirely.

Read the entry →

Kumabe et al. 2025

2025 Human · cynomolgus monkey · mouse Protein DIA proteomics

Deep proteomic profiling of brain microvessels isolated from all three species, alongside five cultured BBB model cell types. Clustering puts human closest to monkey and mouse furthest out — the same ordering this project finds in coding sequence, reached from protein expression instead. Also the primate BBB expression data the pipeline currently lacks, and a clear demonstration that immortalised cell lines drift away from real brain endothelium.

Scientific American: The Healthy Brain

2026 Special edition Popular science

The Fall 2026 special edition, read for a sense of where the field is heading. Sleep and CSF clearance, and a map of brain drug development by treatment target, in which vasculature is one class among a dozen. The lead worth chasing: the leaky sections of the barrier, rather than only the tight ones.
