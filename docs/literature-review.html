Literature Review

Papers on BBB gene conservation, translational models, and comparative genomics — with comments

Author

Yara Ismail

Published

September 13, 2026

Papers are read against six fields, in the order below, so entries stay comparable. The papers the pipeline itself is built on are cited on the Resources page.

How each paper is read

1. Year published
Establishes what was technically possible at the time. A 2010 microarray study and a 2025 deep-proteomics study are not making the same kind of claim.
2. Model(s) used
Which species, whether the comparison is within or across species, and whether the cross-species claim rests on the same tissue.
3. Brain region
Recorded whenever stated. Cerebrovascular endothelium differs between brain regions, and between arterial, capillary and venous segments of the same vessel tree, so a gene “BBB-enriched” in one region or segment may not be in another. Where a paper does not state the region — or where regions are not matched between species — that is recorded too.
4. What is measured
DNA, RNA, or protein. Sequence conservation and expression answer different questions, and a paper measuring RNA cannot on its own support a claim about conservation.
5. How it is measured
Microarray, bulk RNA-seq, single-cell/nucleus RNA-seq, proteomics, genomic sequencing. For RNA-seq, bulk or single-cell, and whether cells were sorted. Bulk brain tissue drowns endothelial signal in neurons; isolated microvessels or sorted endothelium do not.
6. Statistical and modelling techniques
What was done to compare across species — orthologue assignment, differential tests and thresholds, alignment, selection estimates, phylogenetic versus pairwise, multiple-testing correction.

Kumabe et al. 2025

Kumabe H, Masuda T, Ito S, Furihata T, Toda A, Mogi M, Araki N, Ohtsuki S (2025). Proteome profile differences among human, monkey, and mouse brain microvessels and cultured brain microvascular endothelial cells. Fluids and Barriers of the CNS 22:53. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12987-025-00650-z. Open Access.

Field
Year 2025 (published online 30 May 2025)
Models Human, cynomolgus monkey (Macaca fascicularis), mouse (C57BL/6N). Plus cultured cells: MBEC4 (immortalised mouse), hCMEC/D3 and HBMEC/ciβ (immortalised human), primary human BMECs, and HUVEC as a peripheral endothelial comparator
Brain region Not matched between species. Human and monkey: frozen cerebral cortex. Mouse: whole cerebrum. Sub-region and vessel segment not resolved — brain microvessels are isolated as a bulk fraction
Measures Protein (primary). RNA secondary — bulk RNA-seq, cultured cells only, not brain tissue
Method Brain microvessels (BMVs) isolated from frozen brain by homogenisation, dextran centrifugation and glass-bead filtration. Untargeted DIA LC-MS/MS (Orbitrap Fusion Tribrid, and ZenoTOF 7600 for monkey and mouse BMVs); library-free search in DIA-NN 1.8.1, MaxLFQ, 1% FDR. QTAP with isotope-labelled internal standards for SLC43A3. Western blot for enrichment control. Bulk RNA-seq via Galaxy 23.2.1, TPM > 1
Cross-species stats Mouse→human orthologues via Alliance of Genome Resources v8.0.0, pseudogenes removed. Mean ± SD; two-tailed Student’s t-test; one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc; Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p. PCA and hierarchical clustering (SIMCA14). GO enrichment (DAVID 2011). Cross-instrument normalisation by a fitted power equation

What it shows. A deep proteomic profile of isolated brain microvessels from three species — 7,149–8,274 proteins per BMV fraction against 6,657–7,534 in whole-brain lysate — plus profiles of five cultured BBB model cell types.

Enrichment was verified before anything else: claudin-5 (endothelial tight junction) was detected in BMV fractions of all species and barely in brain lysate, while PSD95 (a neuronal marker) appeared in lysates and not in BMV fractions. This is the control that makes the rest interpretable — the fraction really is microvessel, not bulk brain.

Of 12,272 orthologous proteins, 6,845 were identified in all three species. Restricting to BMV-enriched proteins (log₂ BMV/brain > 2.5, adjusted p < 0.05) gives 1,636 in mouse, 1,166 in monkey and 2,155 in human, with 528 shared.

The result that matters most here: PCA and clustering of the BMV-enriched proteome put human closest to monkey, and mouse furthest out, on both the first principal component and clustering distance. The authors note this reproduces the phylogenetic tree.

Species differences in specific transporters:

  • ABCB1/Abcb1a (P-gp efflux) higher in mouse than in primates — human/mouse ratio 0.344
  • ABCG2/Abcg2 higher in monkey — 2.57-fold above human
  • SLC22A8/Slc22a8 and SLC22A6/Slc22a6 detected in mouse only, not in monkey or human
  • SLCO1A2 quantified in human; mouse instead expresses Slco1a4 and Slco1c1
  • SLC43A3/Slc43a3 is primate-specific — present in human and monkey BMVs, absent in mouse. Confirmed by targeted QTAP: 22.4-fold higher in hCMEC/D3 than in mouse MBEC4

In vitro against in vivo: primary cultured human BMECs sat closest to human BMVs, while the immortalised lines clustered nearer to HUVECs — peripheral endothelium — than to primary BMECs. Claudin-5 was not detected at all in hCMEC/D3. ABCC1 was 11.3-fold and SLC29A1 1.54-fold higher in hCMEC/D3 than in BMVs; SLC7A5 was 5.79-fold higher in BMVs. Claudin-5 dominated in vivo, claudin-11 in most cell lines, claudin-12 in MBEC4. MFSD2A (DHA transport) was absent from cultured cells entirely.

How it relates to this project.

  1. Independent, non-sequence support for the macaque result. This project’s clearest finding is that macaque coding sequence sits closer to human than mouse does. Kumabe reaches the same ordering from an entirely different modality — protein expression in isolated microvessels. Two different measurements agreeing is stronger than either alone, and it is worth saying explicitly that they are different claims: conservation of sequence versus similarity of expression.

  2. It is a primate BBB dataset, which this project does not have. The audit records that there is currently no macaque expression evidence at all — every gene on the list earned its place from a mouse study, and the paper logged as the macaque dataset turned out to contain no macaque samples. Kumabe supplies exactly that missing layer, at protein level.

    Caveat that has to be checked before using it: this is cynomolgus macaque (Macaca fascicularis). This project’s macaque genome is rhesus (Macaca mulatta, Mmul_10). They are different species. Close, but not interchangeable without saying so.

  3. The brain-region field earns its place immediately. Human and monkey samples are cerebral cortex; mouse is whole cerebrum. Any mouse-versus-primate difference in this paper is partly confounded with region. The authors are open about their other confounders too — longer post-mortem interval in the human samples (2.5–4 h), and perfusion performed in mice only — and they test the blood-contamination one by re-running PCA with abundant blood proteins removed, which does not change the species grouping.

  4. The same orthologue problem, named out loud. The paper states plainly that human and mouse orthologues do not have one-to-one relationships — SLCO1A2 in human against Slco1a1/1a4/1a5/1a6 in mouse. That is the identical issue behind this project’s one-to-many pairs at 25.8% identity, and behind step5d applying different orthologue rules to the BBB and control sets.

  5. A better human validation set than the one currently used. step4b validates the gene list against a single arterial marker sheet. Kumabe’s BMV-enriched protein lists (2,155 human, 1,166 monkey) are a defined, thresholded, protein-level alternative — and they exist for a primate as well as for human.

Methods glossary

Terms from the papers, explained once.

Immortalised cells
A cell line engineered to divide indefinitely, usually by inserting a gene such as SV40 T antigen or telomerase. Ordinary primary cells taken from tissue divide a limited number of times and then stop, which makes them awkward to work with; immortalised lines are convenient, reproducible and cheap. The cost is that they drift away from the original tissue — which is precisely what Kumabe measures: hCMEC/D3 and HBMEC/ciβ resemble generic vein endothelium (HUVEC) more than they resemble real brain microvessels, and hCMEC/D3 has lost claudin-5 entirely.
Western blot
A method to check whether one specific protein is present, and roughly how much. Proteins are separated by size in a gel, transferred onto a membrane, and probed with an antibody that binds only the target. Here it is a quality control, not a result: claudin-5 present in the microvessel fraction shows the fraction contains endothelium; PSD95 absent shows it is not contaminated with neurons. β-actin is the loading control — it should be equal in every lane, confirming equal amounts of protein were loaded.
DIA — data-independent acquisition
A mass-spectrometry strategy. In the older data-dependent approach the instrument picks the most intense peptides to fragment and measure, so weakly abundant proteins are missed and different runs measure different things. DIA fragments everything in successive mass windows, so the same proteins are measured in every run. That is why this study can compare across species and samples with confidence, and why it reaches 7,000–8,000 proteins.
QTAP — quantitative targeted absolute proteomics
Instead of relative abundance, this measures the actual molar amount of a chosen protein, by spiking in a synthetic version of one of its peptides labelled with heavy isotopes. The known standard and the real peptide behave identically but differ in mass, so the ratio gives an absolute concentration. Used here to confirm the SLC43A3 result independently of DIA.
BCA assay (bicinchoninic acid)
A colour-change assay for total protein concentration in a sample. Used so that equal amounts of protein go into each analysis.
Two-tailed Student’s t-test
Tests whether the means of two groups differ. “Two-tailed” means it tests for a difference in either direction — higher or lower — rather than predicting the direction in advance. Two-tailed is the conservative default.
One-way ANOVA
The extension of the t-test to three or more groups, with “one-way” meaning the groups differ along a single factor — here, species. It answers only “are these groups all the same?”, not which pair differs. Running repeated t-tests between every pair instead would inflate the false-positive rate, which is the problem ANOVA exists to avoid.
Tukey post-hoc test
Run after a significant ANOVA to find which pairs actually differ, while correcting for the fact that you are making several comparisons at once.
Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment
A multiple-testing correction. Testing thousands of proteins at p < 0.05 produces many false positives by chance alone. BH controls the false discovery rate — the expected proportion of claimed hits that are wrong — and is less severe than Bonferroni, which is why it suits large screens. The corrected value is the “adjusted p-value”. This project uses both, in step5g.
PCA — principal component analysis
Compresses thousands of measurements per sample into a few axes that capture most of the variation, so samples can be plotted and compared. When human and monkey sit near each other on PC1 and mouse sits apart, that is a statement about overall proteome similarity.
Hierarchical clustering
Repeatedly joins the two most similar samples until everything is in one tree. The branching order and branch heights show which samples group together and how strongly — independent confirmation of what the PCA shows.
GO enrichment analysis
Gene Ontology assigns standard functional labels to genes and proteins. Enrichment asks whether a list contains more of a given label than chance would give — here, showing that BMV-enriched proteins are disproportionately “transmembrane transporter activity” and “maintenance of blood-brain barrier”. DAVID is the tool that performs this test.
TPM — transcripts per million
A normalised unit of RNA-seq expression, adjusted for both sequencing depth and transcript length so values can be compared between genes and samples.

## Methods glossary

Terms from the papers, explained once.

**Immortalised cells**
: A cell line engineered to divide indefinitely, usually by inserting a gene such as SV40 T
antigen or telomerase. Ordinary primary cells taken from tissue divide a limited number of
times and then stop, which makes them awkward to work with; immortalised lines are
convenient, reproducible and cheap. The cost is that they drift away from the original
tissue — which is precisely what Kumabe measures: hCMEC/D3 and HBMEC/ciβ resemble generic
vein endothelium (HUVEC) more than they resemble real brain microvessels, and hCMEC/D3 has
lost claudin-5 entirely.

**Western blot**
: A method to check whether one specific protein is present, and roughly how much. Proteins
are separated by size in a gel, transferred onto a membrane, and probed with an antibody that
binds only the target. Here it is a quality control, not a result: claudin-5 present in the
microvessel fraction shows the fraction contains endothelium; PSD95 absent shows it is not
contaminated with neurons. β-actin is the loading control — it should be equal in every lane,
confirming equal amounts of protein were loaded.

**DIA — data-independent acquisition**
: A mass-spectrometry strategy. In the older data-*dependent* approach the instrument picks
the most intense peptides to fragment and measure, so weakly abundant proteins are missed and
different runs measure different things. DIA fragments *everything* in successive mass
windows, so the same proteins are measured in every run. That is why this study can compare
across species and samples with confidence, and why it reaches 7,000–8,000 proteins.

**QTAP — quantitative targeted absolute proteomics**
: Instead of relative abundance, this measures the actual molar amount of a chosen protein,
by spiking in a synthetic version of one of its peptides labelled with heavy isotopes. The
known standard and the real peptide behave identically but differ in mass, so the ratio gives
an absolute concentration. Used here to confirm the SLC43A3 result independently of DIA.

**BCA assay (bicinchoninic acid)**
: A colour-change assay for total protein concentration in a sample. Used so that equal
amounts of protein go into each analysis.

**Two-tailed Student's *t*-test**
: Tests whether the means of **two** groups differ. "Two-tailed" means it tests for a
difference in either direction — higher *or* lower — rather than predicting the direction in
advance. Two-tailed is the conservative default.

**One-way ANOVA**
: The extension of the *t*-test to **three or more** groups, with "one-way" meaning the groups
differ along a single factor — here, species. It answers only "are these groups all the
same?", not which pair differs. Running repeated *t*-tests between every pair instead would
inflate the false-positive rate, which is the problem ANOVA exists to avoid.

**Tukey post-hoc test**
: Run *after* a significant ANOVA to find *which* pairs actually differ, while correcting for
the fact that you are making several comparisons at once.

**Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment**
: A multiple-testing correction. Testing thousands of proteins at *p* < 0.05 produces many
false positives by chance alone. BH controls the **false discovery rate** — the expected
proportion of claimed hits that are wrong — and is less severe than Bonferroni, which is why
it suits large screens. The corrected value is the "adjusted *p*-value". This project uses
both, in `step5g`.

**PCA — principal component analysis**
: Compresses thousands of measurements per sample into a few axes that capture most of the
variation, so samples can be plotted and compared. When human and monkey sit near each other
on PC1 and mouse sits apart, that is a statement about overall proteome similarity.

**Hierarchical clustering**
: Repeatedly joins the two most similar samples until everything is in one tree. The
branching order and branch heights show which samples group together and how strongly —
independent confirmation of what the PCA shows.

**GO enrichment analysis**
: Gene Ontology assigns standard functional labels to genes and proteins. Enrichment asks
whether a list contains more of a given label than chance would give — here, showing that
BMV-enriched proteins are disproportionately "transmembrane transporter activity" and
"maintenance of blood-brain barrier". DAVID is the tool that performs this test.

**TPM — transcripts per million**
: A normalised unit of RNA-seq expression, adjusted for both sequencing depth and transcript
length so values can be compared between genes and samples.
