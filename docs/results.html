Headline findings

  1. Macaque is the closest model to human BBB. 96.8% nucleotide identity at the coding-sequence level. For almost every gene, macaque sits above the diagonal — more similar to human than mouse.

  2. Mouse is more divergent (84.1%) but still highly conserved. Mouse models remain biologically defensible, but ~16% sequence divergence is present. Where mouse and human BBB phenotypes differ clinically, this divergence is one possible explanation.

  3. BBB genes are under stronger purifying selection than liver genes — most visibly in the human-macaque comparison (BBB dN/dS 0.152 vs Liver 0.196, p < 10⁻⁶ after Bonferroni correction). The signal is weaker in human-mouse because at ~80 million years, all genes have accumulated extensive change.

  4. Gene duplication produces dramatic divergence. Paralogous mouse genes (e.g. Abcb1a + Abcb1b for human ABCB1) are only ~47% identical to human, vs ~85% for one-to-one orthologues. Effect size is large (rank-biserial r = 0.72).

  5. Conservation and human BBB expression are independent. A gene’s validation score (0–3 datasets) does not predict how conserved it is at the sequence level.

Biological interpretation

Strong purifying selection on BBB genes makes biological sense. The BBB controls what enters and exits the brain — broken transporters or tight junctions would let toxins in or block essential nutrients. Evolution actively removes protein-changing mutations from these genes, more aggressively than for liver genes.

The fact that this signal is most visible in the human-macaque comparison — not human-mouse — reflects evolutionary timescale. Subtle differential selection is detectable when species are close enough that not all genes have saturated their substitutions. At ~80 million years for human-mouse, this signal is lost in the noise.

Findings, re-graded against the audit

The five findings above are stated as originally written. This is what the audit does to each of them.

1. Macaque is the closest model to human BBB Direction holds, number unreliable

As stated: 96.8% nucleotide identity; macaque sits above the diagonal for almost every gene.

% identity was computed in step5c and never recomputed after the codon-aware fix. 224 one-to-one macaque pairs score under 85%, which is biologically impossible — SLC2A1 comes out at 71.4%. That is the longest-transcript heuristic comparing non-corresponding isoforms. Macaque being closer to human than mouse is safe; the figure 96.8% is not.

2. Mouse is more divergent (84.1%) but still highly conserved Number unreliable

As stated: ~16% sequence divergence; mouse models remain biologically defensible.

The same % identity problem, plus the gene list itself is wrong. This figure describes a set that is largely pericyte and developmental-timing genes, not BBB-enriched ones. The claim is not disproven — it has not yet been tested on the right genes.

3. BBB genes are under stronger purifying selection than liver genes Withdrawn

As stated: BBB dN/dS 0.152 vs liver 0.196, p < 10⁻⁶ after Bonferroni correction.

The comparator is not a liver control. 86.6% of it is brain-expressed, with brain and liver medians effectively identical (31.3 vs 31.2). It is a housekeeping set — and housekeeping genes are unusually conserved — so the test compared BBB genes against one of the most conserved groups in the genome and read the result as tissue-specific selection. The statistics are correct; the contrast is not. This claim cannot be made until the control is rebuilt, and step 5d's asymmetric orthologue rule has to be fixed at the same time.

4. Gene duplication produces dramatic divergence Survives

As stated: paralogous mouse genes ~47% identical vs ~85% for one-to-one orthologues; rank-biserial r = 0.72.

This is a contrast within the BBB set, so it does not depend on the liver control, and the effect is far too large to be an artefact of the % identity problem. It is the strongest surviving result in the project — worth stating plainly, because a page about mistakes that reports nothing left standing is its own kind of distortion.

5. Conservation and human BBB expression are independent Uninformative

As stated: a gene's 0–3 validation score does not predict how conserved it is.

The validation score barely carries information. One of its three components read only Yang's arterial sheet; another asked whether a gene was detected at all, which 88.5% of genes pass — and that component alone is why 1,131 of 1,526 genes score exactly 1. A null result against a near-constant variable says nothing in either direction.

Limitations

Methodological caveats

  • Pairwise dN/dS, not phylogenetic. A more rigorous analysis uses PAML codeml on a multi-species alignment with an explicit phylogenetic tree.
  • Longest-transcript heuristic. MANE Select / Ensembl Canonical (step5b_alt_canonical_transcripts.R) are more standard. The canonical FASTAs are already generated.
  • Multiple testing correction was applied to primary tests only. Stratified subgroup analyses (by Daneman filter, validation score, paralogue type) are exploratory.

Biological caveats

  • The starting gene list is enrichment-based. CLDN5 (the canonical BBB tight junction protein) is absent because it failed the differential expression cutoff — it is expressed in all endothelia, not just brain.
  • Liver is one specific control choice. A different control (brain non-BBB genes, genes matched by protein domain) could give a different picture.
  • Coding sequence only. Regulatory regions (promoters, enhancers, UTRs) — where most cross-species expression-level differences live — were deliberately excluded.

## Limitations

### Methodological caveats

- **Pairwise dN/dS, not phylogenetic.** A more rigorous analysis uses PAML `codeml` on a multi-species alignment with an explicit phylogenetic tree.
- **Longest-transcript heuristic.** MANE Select / Ensembl Canonical (`step5b_alt_canonical_transcripts.R`) are more standard. The canonical FASTAs are already generated.
- **Multiple testing correction was applied to primary tests only.** Stratified subgroup analyses (by Daneman filter, validation score, paralogue type) are exploratory.

### Biological caveats

- **The starting gene list is enrichment-based.** CLDN5 (the canonical BBB tight junction protein) is absent because it failed the differential expression cutoff — it is expressed in all endothelia, not just brain.

- **Coding sequence only.** Regulatory regions (promoters, enhancers, UTRs) — where most cross-species expression-level differences live — were deliberately excluded.
